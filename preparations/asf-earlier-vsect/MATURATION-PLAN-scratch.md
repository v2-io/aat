## What the census shows

**1. The integrator role holds three roles that the design keeps apart: author, self-checker and spiker.** "Integrator" revises text, re-derives its own landing, and in the sprint also produced new results. The design's independence rule exists for exactly this split, and the 116-of-116 self-confirmations are what you get when it collapses. The spike template's "spiker ≠ verifier ≠ integrator" names the separation, but no tool enforced it.

**2. One role is spread across many labels, and one label covers two roles.** "Verifier" was typed both for *finding* defects (the checker) and for *confirming a finding answered* (the confirmer). Those are opposite stances: one is briefed to find, the other to compare. The flow review measured the difference: about 43% of independent checks found something, against 3 of 36 confirmations. A role recorded as free text can't keep them apart.

**3. The roles that consumed the most effort were never named.** Relaying, merging and committing filled the lead's day, and they show up only in git. Triage over sources (807 events under two labels) had no record kind, which is why its dispositions became prose. Measuring and system analysis left no trace in vsect at all. Those are exactly the places the retrospective found the costs.

**4. Your two roles are nearly absent from the record.** Steward and decider have one event each. The sprint's process decisions were yours, and they live in prose (reflection 31, INTENT) rather than in decision records.

> [!INSIGHT]
> - The founding-questions test for kinds applies to roles too: a role earns its place when it fails differently and is checked differently. By that test the sprint had about ten roles, but tracked them as labels with no constraints between them. Only one constraint was enforced (a confirmer can't be the reviser), and until yesterday it compared typed names.
> - With identity read from the harness, role constraints become computable. "This rederive's actor revised the text", "this confirmer is the reviser's fork", "this checker read the author's account": each is now a query over keys and lineage, not an honor system.

This bears on your aat-v3 question. A clean start could *declare* its roles at founding, each with what it may and may not do relative to the others, the same way the design declares kinds. Then integrator continuity, checker independence and confirmer-not-reviser would be enforced from the first event instead of reconstructed afterward. Whenever you're ready, I'd like to think that question through with you.
