## The thing I most want to check with Joseph

**Joseph's atom and councils-46's atom are two different definitions, and the switch happened silently at 21:53Z.**

- Joseph: an atom is *content + one or more dimensions + rules*; "usually a `##` section".
- councils-46, one minute later: "The fate unit is your atom, a `##` section." and in the rebuilt diagram: "ATOM … the unit of FATE: can be refuted, superseded or ratified while its siblings stand".

Joseph did not say the atom is the unit of fate. He said ASF's failing was that "the frontmatter made it assume that the records within shared the same fate". That says atoms within an instance have *different* fates from each other. It does not say an atom has exactly *one*. And the material says a `##` section often does not:
- `deriv-stochastic-non-exit` `## Formal Expression` = a setup, a theorem, a "why the natural route cannot work" argument, and a general argument for P=1. The second argument can have a hole while the theorem stands on the third.
- Its `## Claim Record` = three entries, each explicitly with its own register and falsifier; the third is uninstantiated while the first is exact.
- `scope-adaptive-system`: one tagged statement with items (a)–(h′) checked separately by vsect 0.1.
- `impl-persistence-and-limits`: per-paragraph and per-clause bracket labels inside one `##`.

So either (i) the atom is the `##`-section-sized thing Joseph pointed at, and then it is not the unit of fate, and something smaller is; or (ii) the atom is the unit of fate, and then it is usually *not* a `##` section, and the `##` section is a slot (possibly 0..N) or a nested template. councils-46 saw the edge of this and filed it as open question 2 ("each one an atom in a 0..N slot, or atoms nested inside one atom?"). I think it is not a detail. It decides what an atom *is*.

My current lean, to test against the model: Joseph's definition is the better primitive. "Content + its dimensions + its rules" defines the atom by *what is attached to it*, which is the thing he told me today had been the unresolved dissonance for months (dimensions attach to parts, not files). "One fate" is then not the definition but a *quality target*: a well-cut atom is one whose dimensions move together; when a check narrows part of an atom and not the rest, that is evidence the atom wants splitting. That is how ASF actually behaves: the lettered items were one atom until a check bound to "(b)". Grain is discovered by adjudication, not declared up front. PLAN already says this for cycles ("a cycle … is a grain defect") and the feedback says it for chunks ("a chunk with two payloads is a grain error").
