## 1. The set-theory footing

### 1.1 Four sorts of thing in one column

Read the candidates table with RC1 02's objects in hand and the rows sort themselves:

| Sort | Rows | What RC1 calls it |
|---|---|---|
| **speech-act kinds** | statement (and its subkinds), derivation, teaching, decision, finding, question, review, run, purpose | KIND: individuated by failure-and-repair; stable for the record's life |
| **composition shapes** | segment ("yes: statements, derivation and teaching, in an arrangement"), chapter/part/volume/theory, arrangement | the four closure shapes: reify, view-ify, cluster, exempt-visibly |
| **companions** (kinds whose standing needs a target) | derivation *proves* a claim; claim record *describes* a statement; term *coins* and *fits* a usage; teaching and front statement *narrate* | a kind plus a declared edge, with cardinality on the edge (06, 11) |
| **flux objects** | source, chunk | membrane objects, not population kinds; a chunk has a crossing, a record has a fate |

Three consequences for the three doubts:

- *"A definition is a coinage (decided) and also fits usage (evidenced)."* One record, two dimensions. RC1 03 says exactly this ("a **definition** is a coinage (Backing) + fit-to-usage (Evidence)"). Not a facet problem.
- *"A segment is a composite and also has an arrangement."* A segment is a **cluster** (typed parts under one identity: body, Working Notes, Findings, Claim Record, each with its own write semantics) that *reifies* a coherence claim; its section order is an **arrangement** record it holds. Shape and kind are orthogonal, so there is no overlap to resolve.
- *"A finding is a defect report and also an open item."* Two records, by the boundary test (§1.3). ASF's ledger already splits them: the dashboard counts 182 findings and 632 items separately, and the item specimen carries `from: item:…`. The finding is a *claim with a target* (evidenced: a check broke or narrowed something); the item is *work owed* (decided, owned by a KR). The appendix row "finding: disposition, decided" misfiles the finding's truth under its routing.

So: **kinds are a partition of records** (every record has exactly one kind, for its life), and the appearance of non-partition comes from listing dimensions and shapes in the kind column. The fix is columnar, not conceptual.

### 1.2 What a record is, in one line

A record is (kind, dimensions opened, composition shape, edges declared), under a maintained name. Kind fixes failure and repair, write semantics, and ceiling. Dimensions fix tracked state. Shape fixes how it stands in naming (reify: it has a name; view-ify: it is a generator; cluster: inner locations under one name; exempt: outside the match population). Edges fix what it reaches and what reaches it. The appendix's "composite?" column is the shape column, and "main aspects" is the dimensions column; both are right to exist and wrong to be in the kind table's primary key.

### 1.3 The two rules the material already carries

RC1 10's strain record (2026-08-30, steward-surfaced) has two sentences the appendix needs and does not use:

- **The record-boundary question precedes the speech-act question.** *Can this piece fail (be refuted, rot, reopen) while that piece stands?* Fail-together: one record, several dimensions. Fail-apart: separate records. Q1's honest output is a *list* of acts, not a choice.

### 2.3 Epistemic Status is four things, not a projection

SCRATCH §3 says Epistemic Status "is a projection in RC1's terms, so the prose either becomes the declared form or is dropped." Read `scope-adaptive-system`'s and it does four jobs:

1. per-atom tier assertion ("(a)–(h′) and the housing result are *exact*"): a projection, to be computed;
2. provenance of imported machinery ("the Kalman criterion in (b) is standard Gaussian conditioning, the decay rate in (c) is the Clarke–Barron asymptotic, the full weak support … is Ferguson's"): events on the *imported* line (`kinds.yaml`: named → recalled → found-by-search → checked-at-secondary → checked-at-primary);
3. an explicit non-claim ("Not claimed: that the declared world … can be read off the observations"): RC1 03's **claim license**, a declared bound, not a dimension;
4. new mathematics ("If the loop is in at $t$, then under some continuation the state-and-law information terms (ii′) … sum to at least that continuation's value"): a derived statement that must be reified, or it is math living in a projection.

So Epistemic Status is a mixed composite in the wild. The boundary test splits it into a generated projection, imported-line events, one bound, and zero or more statements. `def-chronica`'s is one sentence; `example-kalman`'s is a shape declaration ("a *worked instantiation*, not a theoretical claim"); `sketch-…`'s carries a **ceiling** ("a closed bridge would be `derived (conditional)`…; a clean impossibility would be a no-go"). That last is RC1 Q6 in practice, and FORMAT's "Max attainable: [status]" convention and its "three rings" table are ceilings held in prose and in an SOP rather than on records.

### 3.2 Three things under one heading

Reading those, Working Notes hold three things that fail apart:

1. **Uncrossed chunks: the influx.** Proposals (brief prose, discussion, figures), forward pointers to work nobody has started, candidate experiments, "belongs elsewhere". These are what the appendix's "segment-local influx" reading covers, and the reading is right for them: *anything bearing on this segment collects here and is drained into its records or delivered elsewhere.*
2. **Standing records, mis-shelved.** Regression guards (present-truth no-gos about the record's own history: a decision with falsifiers, or a no-go), open questions with closers (`def-chronica`'s two), agent-proposed conjectures explicitly marked as such ("A conjecture attached, marked as conjecture … Agent-proposed forward pointer, not a steward decision": Q0 standing, in the wild), and dead-end warnings with reasons. These have no drain. FORMAT's "What earns a Working Note" names exactly these three (forward pointers, regression guards, dead-end warnings) as what *stays*. They are population, and the appendix's influx reading does not cover them.
3. **Reader evidence kept after its proposal died.** Both `def-agent-environment` §4 and `scope-adaptive-system` §3 carry "Preserved as a fresh-reader-stumble signal even though resolved in-segment." The chunk had two payloads: a reader reaction (fidelity-fixed testimony, a Comprehension event on the record) and a proposed fix (a proposal, now superseded). PLAN A6's "exactly one current crossing per chunk" is right, and this is the specimen showing the chunk was cut too coarse. The boundary test applies at chunk grain too.

So the candidate reading in the appendix ("every composite has its own local influx; its items are chunks with a target composite") holds for (1), and the macro fix for (2) is to stop calling them Working Notes: a guard is a decision record addressed by the segment; an open question is a question record with `concerns: segment:x`. The 2026-10-30 drain already did this for 64 questions (one specimen: `questions/adversary-optimal-drift-fraction.md`, `asked-by: drain-wn-01-gold-opus-5.5`, citing "WN line 91"). What it could not do is remove them from the WN, which is §3.4.

### 3.3 The in-place markers are the real crossing mechanism

**[measured]** about 30 bullets in 12 segments open with a fate marker (`*Resolved 2026-10-30 …*`, `*Answered 2026-10-30 in #scope-adaptive-system*`, `*Superseded 2026-10-30: …*`, `*Landed 2026-10-30 in Discussion, "Whose uncertainty"*`, `*Dissolved 2026-10-30: …*`, `*Qualified 2026-10-30: …*`, `*Partly landed …*`); `form-composition-closure` has five, the three roots edited on 2026-10-30 have three each. Each marker says the fate, the date, and where the content went, and *leaves the chunk in place* under it. That is a crossing recorded as prose on the chunk, which is the shape A6 wants as an event, and it is also the only place in the corpus where a crossing wrote back to its source.

Note the vocabulary those markers used, because it exceeds A6's seven outcomes: *Answered in #x* (delivered to another record), *Landed in Discussion* (state change on this record, naming the span), *Superseded* (with what now says), *Dissolved* (the question no longer arises because a premise changed: not rejected, not answered), *Qualified* (partially superseded: the chunk's reader reaction stands, its proposal is narrowed), *Partly landed*. The drain files used yet another vocabulary (`canon / superseded / defect → item / owed → item / joined item:x / guard / set aside / in flight`; and `IN / DEFECT / ADD / LEDGER / ASIDE / GUARD / HIST`). Three outcomes recur in practice that A6 lacks:

- **mint**: a new record is made from the chunk (`defect → item`, `owed → item`, `ADD`). "A state change on a record" does not cover making one.
- **stays as record**: the chunk is itself a record of a declared kind and stays where it is (`guard`). This is the terminal that FORMAT's three keep-kinds name.
- **joined as source**: an existing record gains this chunk as one more source (`joined item:x`, `vsect raise`). A6 calls this "duplicate", which loses what the sprint cared about most ("One thread, five independent arrivals: whose quantity is it?", `audits-top-level.md` §1.1). Under the coherence default a second independent arrival is a testimony/instance event on the target, and its descent (`seeded-by`) is what decides whether it adds weight. That is an evidence event, not a duplicate.

### 3.4 Why nothing retires: the influx is inside the record

PLAN A6: "A source retires when every chunk is terminal: the delete-test, computed." The sprint disposed every Working Note in four volumes and retired none, and I.10's measurement says why in numbers: a cold agent's reading per claim was unchanged, "60% of the new coverage is named only in `drain/`, nothing was retired, and 94% of net new places are this effort's own files." The drains were read-only on segments (three lanes were editing them), and "nothing writes the population directly" made the WN section, which is part of the segment's hashed text, unwritable by a crossing. So every `canon` and `guard` disposition left its chunk where it was, and the source could not pass the delete-test.

For the appendix this is the practical-flow finding: **a crossing must write back to its source chunk** (delete it, or annotate it with its fate and target, which is what the 2026-10-30 markers did by hand), and for that to be legal the local influx cannot be inside the record's hashed body. Two designs fit: the WN section is an inner location of the cluster with its own write semantics (append by anyone; delete-on-crossing by the membrane), excluded from the body's version hash; or the local influx is a sidecar keyed to the segment, as Line A did with claim records. Either way, "WN empty at `candidate`" (FORMAT Gate 4) becomes *local influx drained*, computed, instead of a thing an agent remembers.

The sprint's working-notes map also records a side fact worth carrying: **no Working Notes line in any volume changed between 2026-08-24 and the sprint** (its §1, from `git blame`). Half the segments' notes were last touched in May by the lift. A local influx nobody drains for two months is a queue with no drainer, which is the Fable read's "idle stations" condition (asf-flows feedback §3.2), and it is a flow fact the kind list cannot see.

### 3.5 What "drained for a specific segment" means, operationally

Joseph's phrase: an influx "for a specific segment to be drained". Read against the specimens, draining a segment's local influx is one episode with these acts, each of which is a crossing with a write-back:

- a reader-confusion chunk → a Comprehension event on the record, and (by the project's standing rule, agents.sop "Codex open questions are reader-clarity gaps") a body edit; the chunk is deleted or marked *answered in body*;
- a proposal chunk (brief, discussion, figure) → a proposal record against the segment, or a pedagogy-store record (H.4), or rejected with reason; deleted from the WN either way;
- a follow-up chunk → a question (with closer) or an item (with owner), *minted*, and the chunk replaced by its id;
- a "belongs elsewhere" chunk → efflux to the named store, and the chunk replaced by the delivery's id (the gold drain found that "none of it has arrived at its destination" because 02–04 were never lifted; delivery needs a receiving influx, which is A6's "designated influxes");
- an off-ramp chunk → a finding (claim-with-target), verified against the body at a version, then mint or `already present`; the drain notes "A Working Note's 'resolved' or 'routed' label is not trusted in either direction", which is the attested-floor rule applied to the chunk's own self-description;
- a guard or dead-end → declared a record of that kind and *left*, visibly typed, which ends its life as a chunk.

That list is the segment-local half of the macro-structure the appendix says is "still wanting". It is also most of what the 2026-10-30 drains did by hand, minus the write-back.

## 4. Macro-structure: kinds from stations

RC1 06 derives edge kinds from consumers: "an edge kind earns its place exactly when some consuming operation routes it differently." The same rule applied to kinds, *a kind earns its place when some mover minds it differently*, gives the appendix a way to generate the list instead of arguing it. ASF's actual loop, as the sprint and the segments show it, has these stations; each reads certain kinds and mints others:

| Station (as ASF ran it) | Reads | Mints or moves | Specimen |
|---|---|---|---|
| **author** writes a segment | prior statements, the outline | statements (atoms), the cluster, its arrangement, a coinage (term) | any `def-*` |
| **de-novo audit** reads cold, predictions committed first | the bodies, nothing priming | findings (claims-with-target), reader confusions (Comprehension events), residue: proposals, forward pointers | `audits/AUDIT-WORKING-184930/00-initial-predictions.md` (the commitment law, in practice) |
| **gold lift** classifies residue into the target segment's local influx | audit dirs | chunks, under a six-slot dialect; off-ramps routed | 120 segments, 2026-05-30/31 |
| **adjudication / off-ramp** | findings | items with owners; or `already present` | `audits/pending-findings-*`, the off-ramp blocks |

| **confirmer** (≠ reviser) re-binds after rewordings | lapsed checks, the diff | confirm events; `✓~` | dashboard: 223 rewordings awaiting, 336 checks lapsed |

| **term cycle** | usage across segments | canonicalize / rename / alias / nuance / weak events | `terminology/decisions/` |
| **experiment** | a claim and a MANIFEST | runs (seeded, dated, output), consumers edges | `empirica/INDEX.md` |
| **front door / build** | bodies | front statements, generated projections (`FINDINGS.md`, `LEXICON.md`, `CURRENT-VOL1.md`) | `bin/extract-findings`, `bin/term render` |

Three things fall out of looking at it this way:

- **Every kind in the appendix is minted at a station, and several stations mint kinds the appendix lacks:** account (the CHANGELOG narrative, which carries authored judgment the event log does not: "re-checked the substitution's arithmetic, not the substitution"), proposal (a chunk against a record that is neither a finding nor a question), experiment (distinct from run: `empirica` MANIFESTs carry claims, tier, consumers, vivarium status, and may legitimately say "Claims: None yet"), worked example (§5), and objective/KR (§5).
- **Role separation is a kind constraint, not a council.** spiker ≠ verifier ≠ integrator ≠ confirmer is enforced in ASF by identity (`CC_AGENT`; `vsect confirm` refuses the reviser). PLAN A4's "who fills slots is decided later" is right, and the appendix can still record that four of the stations above require *a different actor from the one before*, as the only mover constraint ASF has actually needed.
- **The two idle edges are flow facts the list cannot carry.** Spike → integrator ("the integration step those files were written for never ran", `internal-surfaces/spikes.md` §1) and crossing → write-back (§3.4). A kind list says what records can be; a station table says which edges have a drainer. The Fable read of the flow model asked for exactly this sibling condition ("every queue has a drainer with nonzero service share, or is declared a sink"), and the appendix's "macro-structure" gap is that condition stated as a table.
