There is something even "weaker" than known gap (which doesn't even know how many segments or what kinds will replace it, but which sometimes have to be defined well enough to support something downstream [which will obviously know it's resting on something potentially very unstable-- but maybe it has an idea for how stable it *will* be and it's just a matter of getting to writing those segments-- this is common]): *provisional* missing/promised + gaps. Lots of promised segments named certain things but unwritten, sometimes even given a kind and expected epistemic value/standing -- or a maximum epistemic value (which I think we now see as a poor categorization), when it is essentially a guess for guiding the minds that follows is promising far more than is warranted. ## What I take from it (mine)

- **The inbox belongs in the core**, not in the pattern library.
- **A row of the canonical projection has a commitment state.** *Provisional*: a suggestion that such a segment, or such a gap, is probably wanted. *Promised*: the store now says this will exist. *Present*: it exists. Moving from provisional to promised is a decision someone stands behind.
- **A gap is a kind of segment.** It says what is missing and, where something downstream must rest on it, enough about it to be rested on. It ends by vanishing or by being replaced by one or more real segments. Something resting on a gap is known to be resting on something unwritten, and that shows in what it rests on.
- **This is the store's honesty rule applied to its own structure.** A steady state must be true as stated, with its conditions. An outline full of unmarked unwritten rows promises more than is warranted. The same rows marked provisional are true as stated: they are guesses offered as guidance.
- **There must always be one canonical projection**, however experimental.
- **Why it matters in practice** (his observation): without the mark, the first agents either obey the draft outline too closely or stop telling grounded from ungrounded.
